0. Executive summary

  • Nextdoor is worth testing, but organic-first and cheap. The 2026 platform reality updates your internal runbook's "no paid case" conclusion: self-serve Ads Manager now has native lead-gen forms, ZIP/radius targeting, homeowner/income/age targeting, a Pixel + Conversion API, and no enforced budget minimum (UI recommends $3–$10/day) [live-verified]. A ~$300 paid micro-test is now feasible and readable as a go/no-go screen — but not as a statistical A/B.
  • The consumer track leads; the business track is a cheap side-bet. Everything found says Nextdoor is a consumer-recommendation culture where home-services and "helpful local person" offers thrive. There is no documented precedent for a consumer AI companion sold by a solo founder on Nextdoor — you'd be first (opportunity and warning). SMB owners are a targetable ad audience there (Nextdoor claims 3.2M active), but no evidence exists that businesses shop for B2B services on Nextdoor.
  • Your funnel is not ready for dollar one. The site has zero analytics/tracking of any kind, no UTM capture, no per-offer landing pages, no thank-you/conversion pages, no privacy policy (required for lead-gen ads), Stripe is structurally test-mode, and the consumer product has no self-serve purchase path. Phase-0 organic can start almost immediately; paid spend must wait for the ~2-day build checklist in §7.
  • The single most important strategic call: do not lead with "install my AI companion." Lead with the named per-task hooks (insurance denial, medical bill) that match ask-a-neighbor culture, and sell Mini Jarvis/Jolt as a white-glove, founder-installed "founding neighbor" offer in the background — the proven playbook of your nearest analog (mobile computer repair / senior tech help businesses).
  • Total cash at risk through Phase 2: ≤ ~$1,800, gated at three explicit kill points. Phase 0 is $0.

2. Research findings (six briefs, condensed; full citations in Appendix)

2.1 Nextdoor Ads Manager today

  • Formats: image, video, carousel, native lead-gen ads (2–11 form fields incl. up to 3 custom questions) in the newsfeed [live-verified — creative-specs page].
  • Targeting: ZIP codes (multiple, map preview) or radius, plus homeownership, income, age, gender, interests, custom/lookalike audiences [live-verified — official product page].
  • Minimum budget: no enforced floor found. Nextdoor's own budget guidance presents $3/$5/$10-day tiers with fully custom amounts allowed; UK Dec-2025 launch says "a few pounds" minimum. Exact US floor unverifiable from official pages — confirm by typing $3/day into ads.nextdoor.com (2-minute check) [live-verified guidance / unverified exact floor].
  • Billing gotcha: charged upfront for daily budget × 31 days (or × duration if shorter) [live-verified vendor blog].
  • Tracking: Nextdoor Pixel and Conversion API (online, offline, custom events) exist [live-verified]. UTM parameters pass through but aren't a documented first-party feature [unverified].
  • Policy: no ban on AI products; AI-generated creative must be disclosed [live-verified]. Lead-gen advertisers must have a privacy policy [live-verified]. Insurance/financial-products ads carry strict disclosure rules; an insurance-appeal drafting service isn't literally in that category, but no policy category covers it — a manual reviewer could classify it as insurance-adjacent. Get a written category ruling from Nextdoor ad support before spending on those hooks [assumed risk, reasoned from adjacent policy text].

2.2 Free surfaces

  • Business Page claim + verification (phone or EIN/business documents) is free; verified pages can post to the feed [live-verified/unverified mix — help center was TLS-unreachable to automation].
  • Post frequency conflict: marketing site says "unlimited free posts"; the help center (via snippets) says 2 Business Posts/month; one practitioner blog says 3/week with suspensions. Plan on 2/month until you read the real cap in your dashboard [conflict, explicitly unresolved].
  • Reach: roughly a 2-mile default radius, adjustable, neighborhoods addable/removable [radius mechanic live-verified; "2 miles" unverified].
  • Local Deals moved to ZIP + daily-budget pricing (transition page live); no public rate card; third-party figures ($1–3/day starting) are vendor content [live-verified structure / unverified prices].
  • Neighborhood Faves ran in 2025 (voting closed Oct 17, 2025); free to participate; no 2026 cycle announced yet [live-verified program / unverified date].
  • Hard rule: no business promotion from a personal account in resident feeds; sales content belongs in Business Posts / Local Deals / Events; ownership must be disclosed when commenting about your own business [consistent across sources, primary text unfetchable — verify in-app].

2.3 Cost benchmarks

  • Practitioner CPC ~$2.50–$3.50, baseline CPM ~$20 [agency-sourced, labeled as such]. Meta cross-industry lead-objective CPL $27.66 (WordStream 2025, +21% YoY); Google LSA local-services CPL ~$30–80 [live-verified aggregator reports].
  • The only hard small-dollar Nextdoor self-report is from 2022 ($33/30 days → 4 leads) — stale; don't extrapolate.
  • At $5–10/day you buy ~2–4 clicks/day: delivery is visible fast, lead-level signal takes 2–4+ weeks. Complaints exist of ads "never appearing" at $450/mo (one-sided, unverifiable setup) — check delivery dashboards early.
  • No Houston/Texas-specific Nextdoor cost data exists publicly.

2.4 Audience fit (the honest read)

  • Platform health: WAU 22.3M, +1% YoY — an "all-time high" that is effectively flat; revenue +14% YoY with self-serve ads +28%; near-breakeven EBITDA; July-2025 "Nextdoor Next" redesign (AI recommendations, alerts, local news) is an admission the old format wasn't sticky [live-verified — May 2026 8-K, TechCrunch].
  • The famous "75% homeowners, $90K income" demographics trace only to templated SEO content, not measurement; the last independent study (Pew) is from 2021 [live-verified absence].
  • Consumer AI headwind: consumers saying heavy AI use would hurt trust in a favorite brand doubled 20%→40% between 2025 and 2026 (Fractl survey) [live-verified]; Nextdoor culture measurably skews anxious/guarded (CU Boulder study, Mar 2026) [live-verified]. Counterweight: Nextdoor itself now pushes AI features in-feed, normalizing the word, and the per-task hooks map onto normal "ask neighbors for a specialist" behavior.
  • B2B verdict: SMB owners are on Nextdoor as people (Nextdoor claims 3.2M, 22% professional services, 66% with purchasing control — vendor stats, Jan 2026), but nobody documents B2B services being bought there. Targeting owners-as-residents with ads is untested-but-not-foreclosed; expecting plumber-style discovery for an AI agency is a conflation to avoid.
  • Houston: 1,664 Nextdoor neighborhoods [live-verified]; city runs official agency accounts; no public city-level usage data.

2.5 Analog campaigns

  • No named case study exists of a solo tech/AI consultant winning (or failing) on Nextdoor, and no precedent at all for a consumer AI companion product — green-field, stated plainly.
  • Nearest proven cousins: mobile computer repair / "tech help for seniors" businesses. Their observed playbook: house calls, patient personal manner, years of recommendation-thread accumulation and review stacking — not campaigns [live-verified page observations].
  • Culture-fit patterns Nextdoor itself promotes (vendor content): founder-story posts, credential-led trust posts, "first N neighbors free/discounted" giveaways, community-safety/scam-alert posts, testimonial resharing.
  • Spam pitfalls: "I built something, DM me" pattern-matches historical Nextdoor scam shapes (fake contractors, distress scams — 2019 reporting, old but instructive); moderation is peer-voted and reportedly inconsistent. Incentivized reviews are a ban risk.

2.6 Tiny-budget test methodology

  • Houston metro: 235 ZIPs, 2.66M households (city proper ~96–100 ZIPs) [live-verified]. At ≤$500 total, a matched multi-cell geo experiment is impossible — run at most 2 cells (treatment ZIP-cluster vs a similar comparison cluster).
  • Floor for a directional read: $10–20/day for 2–3 weeks per cell (~$140–420/cell). Nextdoor's own guidance: review every 10 days; a UK practitioner case saw conversions start ~day 10 [live-verified].
  • Classical significance is categorically out of reach: detecting 2%→4% conversion needs ~1,140 clicks/arm ≈ $3,400/arm at $3 CPC [derived-math]. At $150–500/cell expect 1–8 leads. So the micro-test is a binary go/no-go screen, never an A-vs-B percentage claim.
  • Measurement plumbing that works at this scale: UTM-tagged dedicated landing pages (UTMs survive iOS/Safari stripping; click-IDs don't [live-verified]) + Nextdoor Pixel on a real thank-you URL + mandatory "how did you hear about us" intake field (self-report recovers only ~25–40% — supplementary, not primary [live-verified analog]) + one promo code per cell + Zapier/email export of lead forms (native CSV export unconfirmed).
  • The Nextdoor-specific blind spot: DM leakage. Nextdoor actively steers interested neighbors to in-app messages, which bypass pixel/UTM/forms entirely. Route paid CTAs to the landing page, and manually log every DM lead with a source question.
  • Attribution window reportedly 7-day click / 1-day view [moderate confidence — help center unfetchable].

4. Offer design (the three chosen tests)

Offer 1 — "The paperwork fighters" (personal, P1+P2)

  • Hook: "Insurers deny 1 in 5 claims — fewer than 1% of people appeal. I built an AI helper that reads your denial and writes the appeal. Houston-local, real person, in reach." (Post A/B text exists in campaign-kits.md; keep the disclaimers: drafting help, not legal/medical advice; no outcome guarantees — both policy-required and culture-required.)
  • Landing experience: NEW page agentsondemand.tech/neighbors/ (or /helpers/): the two named helpers with the demo cards, founder photo + "I live here" line, one intake form (name, email, which problem, "how did you hear about us" required), UTM capture into the intake payload, thank-you URL for the pixel. No checkout — the reply is a personal email/call from Kevin.
  • Measurable action: qualified intake submission (a real denial/bill described) or a DM that gets manually logged. Phase-1 paid version uses Nextdoor's native lead form with custom question "What did your insurer deny / what's on the bill?"
  • Price handling: organic posts may quote per-task founder pricing in DM (suggest $49–$149/denial per Ladder B; contingency-style for bills); no public price until the operator arms one.

Offer 2 — "Founding neighbor" white-glove Jolt/Mini Jarvis (personal, P3, organic only)

  • Hook: "I built a little AI helper that lives on your computer — it sorts your mail, watches your subscriptions, plans the week. I'm installing it personally for the first 5 Houston households at founding-neighbor pricing, and I don't leave until it's running and you like it."
  • Landing experience: /own/ as-is (waitlist) plus the founder conversation — the real funnel is DM → 15-min call → in-home/remote install appointment. The waitlist page already honestly discloses the ~$20/mo AI-subscription cost; keep that honesty in every post.
  • Measurable action: booked install appointments (target: 5). Payment collected manually (invoice/Stripe payment link once live-mode exists; until then, invoice). Each install trades a small discount for a named testimonial + permission to write it up — this manufactures the missing social proof.
  • Why organic-only: no purchase path, no demo video, product still churning; paid spend against this would burn money to expose known gaps. The 5 installs ARE the test.

Offer 3 — "The $650 bad-review fix" (business, B1)

  • Hook: "One bad review costs a Houston business real money — and 89% of readers judge you by the response. I build a one-time tool that drafts the reply that wins the next customer back. $650 flat, you own it. Built by a Houston founder."
  • Landing experience: NEW page /reviews/ (or reuse /build/ with UTM + anchor): the Review Rescue pitch, a before/after review-reply example, the Apply form pre-selecting the tier, thank-you URL. Paid version: native lead form, custom question "Paste or describe the review that's hurting you."
  • Measurable action: lead-form fill with a real review situation → Kevin's 48h proposal SLA takes over (that pipeline is verified live). Deposit via the existing marquee-code flow once Stripe go-live lands; until then, invoice.

Phase 0 — $0, organic surfaces (weeks 1–6)

Phase 1 — first paid micro-test (~$315, weeks 7–9, gated on Phase 0 SCALE + §7 checklist complete)

  • What runs: ONE segment — whichever produced the Phase-0 signal. Default expectation: personal (P1/P2) if Nextdoor support gives a written OK on the insurance/medical category; otherwise business (B1), which has no policy risk.
  • Design: Nextdoor lead-gen ads, 2 geo cells — Cell A: founder's home ZIP-cluster (3–5 adjacent ZIPs); Cell B: a demographically similar suburban cluster (e.g., a Katy/Cypress or Clear Lake cluster; pick by matching owner-occupancy and income from census quick-check). $15/day, 21 days ≈ $315 total ($157/cell). Two creative variants (stat-led vs identity-led), rotated evenly; judge creative only directionally.
  • Plumbing (all from §7): pixel live, UTM landing page, promo code per cell, mandatory source field, DM leads manually logged.
  • Expected signal quality (honest): ~90–125 clicks total, 1–8 leads. This is a go/no-go screen, not an experiment. Delivery sanity-check at day 10 (Nextdoor's own cadence): if impressions are near-zero, pause and fix targeting/bids before burning the rest.
  • Gates (end of week 9):
  • SCALE if ≥3 qualified leads total at ≤ ~$105/lead (business) or ≥3 qualified at ≤ ~$50/lead (personal) — "qualified" = a human read says the custom-question answer is a real situation.
  • ITERATE ONCE (new creative, same budget) if 1–2 qualified leads or good CTR (≥0.4%) with weak form completion.
  • KILL paid Nextdoor if 0 qualified leads after full duration with confirmed delivery — the channel stays organic-only (your original runbook's position, now evidence-backed), and the budget moves to Meta where CPL benchmarks are known.

Phase 2 — scale test (~$1,200–1,500, 6 weeks, gated on Phase 1 SCALE)

  • Design: winning segment + winning creative, $30/day across 4–6 ZIP-clusters covering the highest-fit Houston areas, 6 weeks (~$1,260). Add the Conversion API if lead volume justifies it; keep Meta running in parallel per the existing channel plan so Nextdoor CPL has a live in-house comparison, not just published benchmarks.
  • Read: now ~25–45 leads are plausible if Phase-1 rates hold — enough for a real CPL number and a first close-rate observation, still not enough for creative science.
  • Gates: keep Nextdoor in the standing mix if its cost-per-qualified-lead lands within ~1.5× your own Meta CPL and at least one lead has become revenue; otherwise retire paid Nextdoor to organic + Local Deals and concentrate paid dollars on the better channel.

8. Risks and the skeptical case, stated plainly

  1. The platform is flat. 22.3M WAU growing 1%/yr; the redesign is a retention rescue. You'd be renting attention on a plateaued network — fine for a $300 test, a reason to cap long-term expectations.
  2. AI distrust is rising into your launch window. The share of consumers saying heavy AI use would damage brand trust doubled year-over-year to 40%. On the most scam-primed social platform in America, "I built an AI" is as likely to pattern-match to threat as to magic. The mitigations — named non-AI-led hooks, founder-in-person, community-safety content — are the plan's spine, not decoration.
  3. No precedent exists. Zero documented analogs for a consumer AI companion on Nextdoor. First-mover here may simply mean the market isn't there.
  4. The consumer flagship can't take money. Jolt/Mini Jarvis has no purchase path, no demo video, terminal-only install, customer-supplied AI credentials, and heavy ongoing churn. Any campaign bigger than "5 white-glove installs" would expose this immediately. The product's honest sellable unit today is Kevin's afternoon plus the software, not the software.
  5. Unit economics of the $99 ticket. White-glove CAC almost certainly exceeds $99. This is only rational as land-and-expand top-of-funnel — if expansion revenue doesn't materialize in the first cohort, the consumer track is a loss-maker and should be judged as marketing spend, not product revenue.
  6. Policy/moderation tail risk. The insurance/medical hooks sit in an undefined ad category; a wrong self-classification can mean account suspension after spend. Peer moderation is inconsistent; one flagged post can chill the whole neighborhood presence. Mitigation: written ruling first, Business Posts not personal-feed promotion, disclaimers everywhere.
  7. Measurement will be leaky no matter what. DMs bypass everything; self-report recovers a quarter to a fraction of true attribution; 1–8 leads per cell cannot support fine-grained conclusions. Every Phase-1/2 read is directional. The gates are written to need only counts (0 / 1–2 / 3+), which tiny samples can honestly support.
  8. Solo-operator time is the real budget. Phase 0 costs ~2–4 hrs/week of Kevin-time (posts, replies within hours, DMs, installs). If that time isn't protected, the channel fails independent of everything above — on Nextdoor, the responsive local person is the product.
